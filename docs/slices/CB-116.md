## Goal

Establish one commerce-owned committed-payment truth resolver that completely reconciles the durable
payment faces before returning a replay or integrity conclusion, while preserving each caller's
existing authorization-visibility and existence-concealment boundary. Payment start, authenticated
callbacks, and evaluation state/audit consume the same durable classification without forcing public
and internal callers to expose the same HTTP status.

## In scope

- Define the complete committed-payment durable closure over callback, payment attempt, order,
  payment-ledger, and evaluation-audit faces. The order face spans both standard and seckill order
  tables.
- Enumerate each face by stable or relation keys before asserting status, type, ownership, intent,
  sandbox, or content. Reconcile face cardinality as `0`, `1`, or `>=2`, then validate every
  participating content column and declared invariant.
- Provide one shared immutable committed-payment truth result consumed by payment-start replay,
  production and evaluation callback replay, and evaluation state/audit. Callers may adapt that
  result only through their declared trust boundary.
- Define executable caller metadata for canonical request locators, ownership-bearing visibility
  locators, concealed response family, committed resolver, result type, trust boundary, and whether
  committed truth must precede mutable liveness.
- Apply two-stage classification to owner-concealing public callers: first establish
  `VISIBLE`/`CONCEALED`/`INDETERMINATE` through the caller's canonical owner-scoped lookup; only a
  `VISIBLE` target may be classified `COMPLETE` or `DAMAGED`.
- Preserve unknown/other-owner response parity when canonical visibility cannot prove ownership.
  Do not add broad fallback queries, cross-owner counts, internal reason fields, or stable public
  differences to discover that a concealed row is damaged.
- Keep authenticated callback and sandbox-wide evaluator callers integrity-visible within their
  established correlation, event, or sandbox truth scope. Damaged committed closure remains a
  durable-integrity conflict for those callers.
- Classify every result with a bounded server-only attribution that is available to logs and tests
  but absent from the public response body.

## Acceptance criteria

1. One shared resolver enumerates the five committed faces by stable/relation keys, validates
   cardinality and full participating content, and returns an immutable classification. No caller
   maintains a narrower competing definition of a face.
2. The order face includes `standard_order` and `seckill_order`; a same-`order_id` cross-table
   sibling is inside the cardinality closure and cannot split callback/state/audit classifications.
3. Missing, duplicate, cross-sandbox, cross-type, contradictory, or per-column damaged durable
   truth is never silently filtered out. Enumerators use keys; validity predicates are assertions.
4. Exact committed replay is resolved before mutable sandbox liveness. A changed or unavailable
   liveness value cannot override a complete already-committed result or convert damaged committed
   truth into authorization rejection.
5. A public caller returns durable-integrity conflict only after its canonical owner-scoped lookup
   proves the target visible. If every stable ownership proof required by that lookup is damaged,
   unknown, other-owner, and damaged-current-owner fixtures produce the same bounded concealment
   response and lookup shape with zero effects.
6. Visibility-observation failure is `INDETERMINATE`, not concealment, conflict, absence, or success.
   Dependency/resource failure retains the existing retryable or unavailable classification.
7. Production/evaluation callback and evaluation state/audit callers keep their authenticated
   internal truth scope and classify damaged committed closure as conflict. Public concealment does
   not downgrade internal integrity detection.
8. Canonical payment intent comparison includes every content-bearing field. Same intent returns
   the complete existing result; changed intent is an idempotency conflict and writes nothing.
9. Every committed-face fault cell drives all applicable callers. Its expected result is computed
   from executable visibility metadata followed by the shared integrity resolver; no named-fault
   exception, skipped/N/A concealed cell, or caller-specific durable shortcut is permitted.
10. Classification has unique server-only attribution for confirmed concealment, intent conflict,
    durable-integrity damage, sandbox authorization/liveness rejection, indeterminate observation,
    dependency unavailability, and unexpected failure. Public bodies reveal none of those details.
11. Replaying any damaged cell produces no new attempt, callback, order, ledger, audit, refund,
    Outbox, PendingAction, or ActionReceipt effect. Restoring the fixture restores every caller's
    normal result.
12. No refund contention recovery, direct-refund response change, PendingAction/ActionReceipt,
    generalized lock protocol, schema migration, or later-slice behavior is introduced.

## Rejection paths

- Reject face enumeration filtered by validity, type, owner, status, intent, or sandbox content;
  first-row wins behavior; unchecked duplicate cardinality; and separate caller definitions of the
  same durable face.
- Reject a public fallback lookup that searches another owner, counts broader rows, adds response
  detail, or otherwise distinguishes a damaged concealed target from unknown or other-owner truth.
- Reject treating `CONCEALED` as N/A or skipping it from the fault matrix, and reject returning 404
  for every damaged public target when canonical visibility still proves ownership.
- Reject using internal state/audit correlation to expand a public caller's authorization
  visibility, or reducing authenticated callback/evaluator integrity conflicts to not-found for
  cosmetic cross-caller uniformity.
- Reject mutable liveness before committed truth, observation failure as absence, incomplete
  canonical intent comparison, unbounded parsing/materialization, or raw repository exceptions
  escaping as 500.
- Reject any new payment/refund write, retry protocol, state transition, migration, table/index
  ownership, Action behavior, or global lock-order mechanism.

## Out of scope

- physical MySQL lock-wait bounds and refund contention recovery owned by CB-117;
- PendingAction prepare, confirmation, one-time consume, refund execution, and ActionReceipt owned
  by CB-118;
- global acyclic lock protocols, refund state-machine redesign, payment-provider integration,
  settlement, chargeback, or a generalized reconciliation framework;
- agent confirmation/projection/turn work, memory, handoff, failure export, web, observability,
  deployment, real-provider access, and measured performance.

## Required evidence

- executable committed-face definition and caller-visibility inventories that fail when a face,
  physical table, caller, trust boundary, or required locator is unregistered;
- real MySQL data-consistency fault injection for all five faces, cardinality `0`/`1`/`>=2`, and
  every participating content column, including cross-table order siblings and multi-operation
  audit sessions;
- single and pairwise fault evidence across payment start, production/evaluation callbacks, state,
  and audit, with the oracle mechanically derived from visibility then integrity;
- paired unknown, other-owner, and damaged-concealed public fixtures proving equal status, bounded
  body/schema, canonical lookup shape, no internal detail, and zero effects;
- visible-damage regressions proving owned targets still reach the complete resolver and return
  durable-integrity conflict;
- exact committed-before-liveness, canonical-intent replay/conflict, indeterminate observation,
  dependency-unavailable, rollback, concurrency, and restored-fixture evidence;
- complete-diff self-review against every applicable recurring defect class, fresh independent
  read-only review, final-head CI, the unique closeout `make ci`, and factual completion evidence.

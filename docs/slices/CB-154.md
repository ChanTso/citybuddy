## Goal

Publish independently reproducible RAG HitRate@5/MRR, FAQ mapping and answer hit rates,
Elasticsearch knowledge-search avoidance, and evidence-based first-bottleneck metadata. Produce the
final five-output portfolio aggregate by digest-reference to the already committed CB-152 and
CB-153 bundles, never by recalculating or rewriting them.

## In scope

### Offline RAG quality

- Commit a bounded, versioned public/synthetic labelled dataset with explicit query version,
  relevance-judgement version, corpus/index version, fixed top-five retrieval inputs, and raw ranked
  results for every query. No private or user-derived content is allowed.
- Freeze one query identifier per semantic question. Dataset validation normalizes query text with
  Unicode NFKC, case-folding, and whitespace collapse; exact canonical duplicates fail. It then
  forms Unicode-code-point trigrams with boundary markers and rejects cross-partition pairs whose
  trigram Jaccard similarity is at least `0.85`. Include declared hard negatives that share terms
  but are not relevant; they are checked by the same identity/version rules.
- A malformed, duplicate, stale-version, empty-query, or unjudged row fails the complete evaluation.
  A judged query with no returned document remains in the denominator with reciprocal rank zero and
  no hit; an explicitly judged no-relevant-document query belongs only to the negative-control
  report and never enters the positive-query HitRate/MRR denominator.
- For positive judged queries `Q`, `HitRate@5 = sum(1 when any relevant document is ranked 1..5 else
  0) / |Q|`. `MRR = sum(1 / rank of the first relevant document within ranks 1..5, else 0) / |Q|`.
  Relevance is keyed to the declared source/document identity and version; duplicate returned
  identities fail rather than receiving two ranks.
- The checker reconstructs both metrics from the labelled rows and raw rankings. Service metrics,
  screenshots, prose judgements, and hand-edited aggregates have no authority.

### Blocking cache/Elasticsearch core

The core validity layer is fixed before execution and cannot be reduced after failure:

| Case | Required observation | Denominator rule | Elasticsearch reconciliation |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold eligible | predeclared mapping miss or declared mapping result; answer miss or declared result | reached mapping/answer hit or miss enters its layer denominator | exactly one logical Elasticsearch search is issued |
| warm eligible | mapping hit and answer hit | both hits enter their respective denominators | zero logical Elasticsearch searches; exactly one `cache_served` decision |
| bypass | separately reported | excluded from mapping and answer hit/miss denominators | if a logical search is invoked, exactly one `elasticsearch_issued` |
| unavailable | separately reported | excluded from mapping and answer hit/miss denominators | an invoked logical search counts once; no invocation contributes no backend decision |
| invalid | separately reported | excluded from mapping and answer hit/miss denominators | an invoked logical search counts once; no invocation contributes no backend decision |

The exact formulas are:

```text
mapping hit rate = mapping:hit / (mapping:hit + mapping:miss)
answer hit rate = answer:hit / (answer:hit + answer:miss)
Elasticsearch avoidance = cache_served / (cache_served + elasticsearch_issued)
```

Core requests run serially with one outstanding request. For each predeclared request, capture
before/after deltas from
`citybuddy_agent_faq_cache_lookups_total` and
`citybuddy_knowledge_backend_decisions_total`, plus a sanitized logical Elasticsearch invocation
record from a runner-owned loopback proxy placed on the real Agent-to-Elasticsearch URL. The proxy
is a direct child with no persistent state and records only case window, method, alias path, bounded
request-shape class (`bm25` or `dense`), response class, and monotonic ordering; it never records
query text, response bodies, identities, or credentials. The existing one logical knowledge search
normally produces the ordered BM25 and dense `POST /knowledge_docs_read/_search` pair. A deliberate
invoked-then-failed case may stop after the first observed request, but the serialized public case,
backend-decision delta, and proxy window still prove one logical invocation entered and failed.
Every `cache_served` reconciles to an empty proxy window. Every `elasticsearch_issued` reconciles to
one nonempty logical window and never a second window; invoked-then-failed still counts as issued.
Replay, malformed/rejected input, or budget/circuit denial before the backend choice adds no backend
decision. Provider-attempt counters remain diagnostics only and cannot enter any formula or support
a model-call-saving claim.

The blocking checker requires cold miss/warm hit behavior, exclusion of bypass/unavailable/invalid
from hit/miss denominators, exact counter deltas, zero/one logical-search reconciliation, and raw
request-level reconstruction. Failure blocks CB-154; it cannot be registered as residual.

### Predeclared extended coverage

`expiry` and `replay` are separate extended cases. They must report exact denominator behavior, no
double counting, and no false saving. If either remains unclosed after the two allowed scope-owned
semantic recovery cycles, do not add a third lane or modify the core formulas. Instead retain the
raw failure and set exactly:

```text
workloadScope = cold/warm eligible synthetic workload
expiryCoverage = residual
replayCoverage = residual
```

Only the measured cold/warm synthetic workload may then be claimed; no text may generalize it to
all scenarios. A core-layer failure still marks CB-154 `BLOCKED` and supplies no corresponding
number.

### First bottleneck and five-output aggregate

First-bottleneck analysis consumes CB-152/153 client latency, errors and throughput, authoritative
durable reconciliation, and recorded CPU, memory, I/O and client observations. It may also use
Agent metrics. It lists all evidence and the consumed artifact digests. If the evidence does not
uniquely identify the earliest limiting resource or boundary, the result is `indeterminate` and
explains why; guessing or adding Java instrumentation is forbidden.

The final aggregate contains exactly these five numbered outputs:

1. seckill QPS / concurrency correctness
2. Agent-path P99
3. RAG HitRate@5 / MRR
4. FAQ mapping / answer hit rates
5. Elasticsearch knowledge-search avoidance

First bottleneck is explanatory metadata, not a sixth output.

The first two entries reference the committed CB-152 and CB-153 `result.json` plus their verified
SHA-256 digest. CB-154 validates schema, slice id, digest, and artifact inventory, but never
recalculates, copies into a new authoritative value, edits, or makes those earlier results invalid.
Missing/invalid earlier bundles block the aggregate but do not revoke their independent validity.

### Ephemeral thin runner and publication

The cache measurement uses the common one-command thin lifecycle:

```text
make init-local ENV_FILE=<run-env>
make up ENV_FILE=<run-env> COMPOSE_PROJECT_NAME=<unique-run-project>
start only required application child processes
readiness -> fixed cache workload -> reconciliation -> offline RAG -> artifact write
stop owned child processes
make reset-local CONFIRM_RESET_LOCAL=1 \
  ENV_FILE=<run-env> COMPOSE_PROJECT_NAME=<unique-run-project>
exact residue checks
```

`make up` already owns local-env validation, RocketMQ store/init/probe, data services/readiness,
grants, and Auth/Commerce/Agent migrations. `make down` preserves volumes and `ENV_FILE` and is not
final cleanup. The runner uses a unique Compose project and env file, restricted temporary
credentials, and a trap limited to its direct children. It keeps no cross-command active state,
preserves the first failure if cleanup fails, and mechanically proves exact-project
containers/networks/volumes and owned processes are zero and generated env/secret files absent. It
does not use host prune, the default project, user `.env`, wildcard cleanup, persistent active-run
or phase/PID/container state, cross-command resume, generalized reset/demo/fault semantics,
`make demo-*`, or PR #75 code/schema.

Publish `evidence/measurements/CB-154/manifest.json`, `result.json`, `checksums.sha256`, and `raw/`
under a 25 MiB uncompressed raw budget. If the declared workload exceeds the budget, shorten it
before execution; do not sample away observations. The manifest contains `schemaVersion`,
`sliceId`, `codeRevision`, `environment`, `machine`, `containerResources`,
`fixtureOrDatasetVersion`, `tool`, `toolVersion`, `warmup`, `measuredDuration`,
`concurrencyOrWorkload`, `sampleCount`, `commands`, `artifactInventory`, and `cleanupResult`, and no
secret/token/key, raw identity, session/trace/turn/PendingAction id, production URL, or mutable
absolute temp path. `result.json` contains HitRate@5, MRR, mapping hit rate, answer hit rate,
Elasticsearch avoidance, first bottleneck or `indeterminate`, and five-output aggregate references.
Raw artifacts are lossless, sanitized, bounded, include failures, reconstruct results, and are
committed with them; screenshot-only and hand-edited evidence is invalid.
JSON readers reject duplicate keys, unknown schema fields, malformed values, and declared
size/record-count overflow before materializing the complete artifact set.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The fixed labelled dataset, judgements and raw rankings reconstruct HitRate@5/MRR exactly and
   pass duplicate, near-duplicate, hard-negative, malformed, version, empty, and judgement rules.
2. The blocking cache core mechanically proves cold and warm behavior, exact exclusion of bypass,
   unavailable and invalid from the two hit/miss denominators, and zero/one Elasticsearch-call
   reconciliation for every backend decision. No hand-edited number or provider-attempt formula is
   accepted.
3. Expiry/replay are separately reported. Only after two exhausted semantic recovery cycles may
   either use the frozen residual fields and cold/warm workload scope; the core remains blocking.
4. The aggregate has exactly five outputs, validates and references CB-152/153 committed digests,
   and never rewrites their values or gates their validity.
5. First bottleneck is evidence-backed or exactly `indeterminate`, with the consumed artifacts and
   CPU/memory/I/O/client/error evidence listed.
6. Exact residue checks pass after explicit `reset-local`; every raw observation and failure is
   retained within the declared bundle inventory and budget.
   All tokens, cache TTLs, dataset versions, and other time-bounded fixtures remain valid through
   their final assertion; the separate expiry case never relies on accidental core-fixture expiry.
7. After the first complete evidence run, at most two scope-owned semantic recovery cycles are
   allowed. Each is unique evidence, minimum correction, targeted verification, then full
   evidence/review. Read-only preflight, attribution without correction, superseded CI, approved
   external-infrastructure rerun, and denied-before-execution commands do not count. Core failure
   after the limit marks CB-154 `BLOCKED`; no third framework lane or weakened truth is allowed.
8. CB-154 `VERIFIED`, raw reconstruction, independent `NO BLOCKER`, and exact-main checks complete
   the current CityBuddy route with no `READY` or `IN_PROGRESS` slice and no CB-155.

## Rejection paths

- Reject mutable/private/unjudged datasets, silent drops, duplicate ranked identities, screenshot or
  prose-only evidence, hand-edited aggregates, stale index/corpus versions, and hidden failed cases.
- Reject hit-rate denominators containing bypass/unavailable/invalid, backend decisions without
  exact request/search-call reconciliation, double-counted replay, false cache saving, or any
  provider/model-saving formula.
- Reject CB-152/153 values copied, recomputed, changed, or made contingent on CB-154; reject an
  aggregate with missing/invalid digests or other than exactly five outputs.
- Reject bottleneck guesses, new Auth/Commerce/Java instrumentation, performance optimization,
  `make down` final cleanup, persistent lifecycle frameworks, PR #75 reuse, residue, or a new
  measurement platform.

## Out of scope

- new load or latency measurement, optimization, Java metrics, general demo/fault harness,
  real-provider or production claims, deployment, dashboards, or new business behavior;
- Agent successful confirmation/receipt, memory, handoff, export, blocked/deferred features,
  private-core updates, ServiceEval updates, or any CB-155.

## Required evidence
